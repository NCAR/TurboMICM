# R5. Alternatives to Rosenbrock methods: multistep and iterative schemes, QSSA, IMEX/partitioned, reduced precision

Literature review for the Tier 3 integrator (about 200 species, 550 reactions, 5–30 min split steps, cheap sparse LU, 2–3 significant digits). Compiled 2026-10-18. Bracketed numbers are source numbers from Part 2, which also says what I actually read (**full** = full text, **abs** = abstract only, **cit** = seen only as a citation). "Digits" are significant digits in Sandu's measure, where 2 digits = 1 %. *(inference)* marks my own reasoning, not a source's claim.

## Part 1. Findings

### 1. TWOSTEP (BDF2 + Gauss–Seidel)

- **Scheme.** Variable-step BDF2, y^{n+1} = ŷ + γτ f(y^{n+1}), with f = P(y) − L(y)y and L diagonal, so y = (I + γτL(y))⁻¹(ŷ + γτP(y)). Gauss–Seidel sweeps over the species in a fixed order update each with one scalar division: no Jacobian, matrix or LU, and step changes cost nothing [1, 2, 5]. Restarts use backward Euler; growth after a restart is capped at 2× [5].
- **Sweeps.** The 1994 prototype iterated to a tolerance (at least 2 sweeps, Aitken extrapolation), averaging about 5 sweeps per step [1]. Later codes fix the count: 1 (rural) or 2 (urban) [2]; 2 [5, 6, 8]; 4 for a chemistry–diffusion column [4]. CHIMERE offers 1 or 2, recommends 2, and says this about doubles the time [10]. Extra sweeps only raise accuracy to what BDF2 gives on the same steps [2]. Species order, and lumping or group iteration of NOy/O3, matter with few sweeps but are mechanism-specific [2, 3, 5].
- **Accuracy (20 species, 25 reactions).** Digits (steps) for 1 / 2 / 5 sweeps: TOL 1e-1: 1.34 / 1.82 / 2.24 (59); TOL 1e-2: 1.96 / 2.91 / 3.25 (132); TOL 1e-3: 3.32 / 3.83 / 4.10 (362) [2]. So 2–3 digits cost 130–360 steps. The designers thought more than 1 % redundant [1, 2].
- **Cost.** About 3× faster than DASSL near 1 % (DASSL: 25 Jacobians, 99 backsolves, 69 steps) [1]. Near 1 % comparable to sparse VODE on 66-species EMEP chemistry and slower above it, since VODE uses higher orders; with about 20 species sparsity hardly pays [3]. In a coupled 66-species × 40-level chemistry–diffusion column (band dimension 2640, bandwidth 133), 4 sweeps were 4–5× more efficient than modified Newton with a band solver [4]; coupling destroys sparsity, so a chemistry-only box differs *(inference)*.
- **Benchmark verdict (7 box problems, 15–84 species).** Best of five explicit solvers, and efficient when less than 1 % accuracy is needed. At 2 digits sparse RODAS was fastest in most cases; on the 84-species mechanism all sparse implicit solvers beat all explicit ones except TWOSTEP–Seidel [5]; within 4 digits Rosenbrock was the most cost-effective [6]. TWOSTEP fails on gas–liquid chemistry (10.3 h estimated for 5 simulated days against under 10 s) [5].
- **Large steps.** At fixed 10 and 20 min steps ROS2 was accurate; TWOSTEP (2 sweeps) was accurate on CBM-IV only at 10 min and needed smaller steps on RIVM chemistry, while ROS2 cost only about 2× more [8]. A prescribed minimum step (0.01 s Jacobi, 0.1 s Seidel) made 2 sweeps converge badly [5].
- **Use.** EMEP MSC-W solves its chemistry with TWOSTEP (section 7.8) [9]. CHIMERE's solver is "adapted from the second-order TWOSTEP algorithm", praised for stability at long steps and simple adjoint code [10]. LOTOS-EUROS: not confirmed; the 2017 model paper does not mention it [11].

### 2. EBI and its automation

- **Method.** Hertel et al. (1993) compared EBI with QSSA on CBM-IV against LSODE: at 50 s steps both worked, at larger steps EBI was better, and QSSA without iteration was poor (abstract only) [12]. It iterates backward Euler with linear operators only (mass-conserving), accelerated by exact solutions for groups of species [7], and is "intertwined with the chemistry" [6].
- **Groups.** CMAQ's EBI solves four groups analytically: {NO, NO2, O3, O(3P), O(1D)}, {OH, HO2, HONO, HNO4}, {PAN, C2O3}, {NO3, N2O5}, plus a numerical iteration for the rest; MEBI uses Newton–Raphson for the first two [13, 15]. The stated reasons: "for speed, coupled species groupings are solved together" [15], in a solver built for the Ox, HOx, NOx–NOy and VOC cycles [13].
- **CMAQ speed and accuracy.** SAPRC99, CONUS 32 km (178×124×21 cells), 1.4 GHz Pentium III, CPU h/day: SMVGEAR 16.7, QSSA 8.6, MEBI 5.1, EBI 4.0. Mean relative differences (%, C > 1e-6 ppm; O3 / NO / NO2 / HNO3): QSSA vs SMVGEAR 1.15 / 4.72 / 6.25 / 2.88; MEBI vs SMVGEAR 0.12 / 3.13 / 0.99 / 0.45; EBI vs MEBI 0.02 / 0.36 / 0.54 / 0.22 [15]. CMAQ's 2004 notes rank SMVGEAR < ROS3 < MEBI < EBI ("significantly faster"); EBI/MEBI are "somewhat less accurate", mostly at low concentrations, and unsuitable for reaction-rate budgets; ROS3 (rtol 1e-3, atol 1e-7 ppm) beat QSSA and SMVGEAR (1e-3, 1e-9 ppm), and QSSA was dropped [14]. Huang & Chang (2001) ranked the Hertel solver best and most robust under operator splitting (abstract only) [16].
- **Academic comparison.** On an 18-species model EBI (8 iterations, steps of 13–40 min) was robust at very large steps but could not match RODAS3; its first-order Euler base limits accuracy, and a BDF2 variant was suggested [6].
- **Automation.** CMAQ's `create_ebi` generates solver source from the mechanism data module but does not discover groups: the 13 species roles above are fixed and the user maps names to roles. The mechanism must contain given reactions (e.g. NO2 → NO + O3P, HONO ⇌ OH + NO, HNO4 ⇌ HO2 + NO2), O(1D) losses must be first order, negative product coefficients are allowed only for PAR, and the README says not to use the solver if checks fail [13]. Built for cb6, saprc07t and racm2 [13]; unavailable for RADM2 in 2004 [14]. I found no automatic grouping method.

### 3. QSSA and hybrids

- **Formula.** y(t+τ) = e^{−τL}y + (I − e^{−τL})L⁻¹P: exact for constant P and L, explicit, cost like a low-stage RK [2]. Plain QSSA is order 1; a second "iterated" pass paid only at large tolerances [7]. Extrapolated (2 function evaluations) and symmetric (3) QSSA are order 2 when non-stiff but only shrink the error constant when stiff [7]; extrapolation bought about one digit at equal CPU [5].
- **Hybrids.** DAE-QSSA: τ > 100h explicit Euler, τ < 0.1h steady state, else exponential [7]. CHEMEQ: τ < 0.2h steady state, τ > 5h non-stiff predictor–corrector, else a stiff formula [5, 7]. Young & Boris (1977) and Mott, Oran & van Leer (2000; alpha-QSS, CHEMEQ2) were not read [17, 18].
- **Accuracy limits.** Plain QSSA at implicit-sized steps errs well above 1 %, so tuning and lumping were needed; classic advice is steps of at most 30 s for urban air; EMEP's scheme "can become unstable" in some conditions [2]. Relative errors can be large while absolute errors stay small, peaking at sunrise and sunset [7]. CMAQ's QSSA differed from SMVGEAR by 1–6 % [15].
- **Versus implicit.** QSSA was the slowest at equal accuracy on CBM-IV [5]; TWOSTEP is best at 2 digits and sparse VODE at 2.5 or more, QSSA variants behind both [7]. All explicit solvers fail when stiffness is not tied to short-lived species (gas–liquid: QSSA estimated at 350 days for 5 simulated days) [5]. In astrophysical networks QSS methods rival implicit ones far from equilibrium but not near it [19].

### 4. BDF, NDF and general implicit codes

- **SMVGEAR.** Gear's 1971 code plus sparse LU (species ordered by production/loss term count, no pivoting), vectorized over blocks of grid cells. Sparse LU needs 20× (order 40) to 120× (order 90) fewer decomposition operations than a full matrix and 5–12× fewer back-substitution operations; there are 3–12 back-substitutions per decomposition. It took 1–2 min per simulated day for 92 species, 222 reactions and 10,000 cells on one Cray C90 processor, was about 60× faster than LSODES in large domains (mostly vectorization; slightly faster unvectorized) and 30–55× faster than the authors' MIE scheme (iterated backward Euler feeding a forward Euler) [20]. SMVGEAR II reorders cells by stiffness each interval, "a factor of more than two" faster [21]; recomputing the absolute tolerance often cut time 50–67 % (urban) and 14–44 % (global) [22]. CMAQ defaults: rtol 1e-3, atol 1e-9 ppm [14].
- **NDF over BDF.** BDF_k plus a κ(∇^{k+1}y) term, κ = −0.1850, −1/9, −0.0823, −0.0415, 0 for orders 1–5: about 26 % larger steps at equal accuracy for orders 1–3 (12 % at order 4, none at 5), at a stability-angle cost (86° to 80° at order 3, 73° to 66° at order 4). In ode15s on 13 stiff problems NDFs took 10.9 % fewer steps and ran 8.2 % faster [23]. No atmospheric code using NDFs found.
- **Newton cost versus fixed solves.** ode15s keeps a Jacobian copy, refactors when step or order changes, and abandons the chord iteration if it will not converge in 4 iterations; its Rosenbrock sibling ode23s forms a new Jacobian every step and is "especially effective at crude tolerances, when a one-step method has advantages" [23]. KPP's Rosenbrock methods need one LU per step and 2–5 function calls (ROS-2 2, ROS-3 2, ROS-4 3, RODAS-3 3, RODAS-4 5) [25]. In GEOS-Chem (106 species, 311 reactions), per advection step and cell, Rodas3 averaged 9 function calls and 3 steps against 39 calls and 28 steps for SMVGEAR II, and was about 2× as efficient at moderate accuracy [24]. LSODE with KPP sparse routines gave 3–4 digits in about 300 s ("upper left corner" of the work–precision plot), but the authors dropped it because it did not reach its asymptotic regime at their tolerances [24]. Restarts hurt multistep codes (VODE stayed uncompetitive at low accuracy despite 2–3× from sparse algebra [7]) and favor one-step RODAS [6].
- **Start-up.** Cutting the restart interval from 60 to 15 min (4× more restarts, starting step also lowered from 60 s to 0.1 s) made all solvers 3–4× costlier, "most of their time in the start-up phase" [6]. Verwer et al. argue for large a priori steps in operator splitting, which avoid parallel load imbalance and interference with vectorization over the grid [8].

### 5. IMEX, partitioned and multirate

- **IMEX BDF2.** Advection explicit, chemistry and vertical diffusion implicit: the partition is by process time scale, not species [4]. Wolke & Knoth's IMEX Runge–Kutta likewise treats chemistry and vertical transport implicitly (second-order BDF) and horizontal advection explicitly, as used in MUSCAT; I know this only from search summaries of the abstracts [26, 27].
- **MOZART and CAM-chem.** The preprocessor generates one explicit and one "semi-implicit" solver, and the user assigns each species by lifetime. Explicit species are long-lived (N2O, CFCs, CH3Cl, CH3Br, Bry, Cly); the semi-implicit solver is recommended unless a species is long-lived everywhere [29]. The numerics are in the auxiliary material of Kinnison et al. (2007), which I could not obtain [30]. No measured gain is reported.
- **Automatic partitioning.** Santillana et al. (2010): each step and cell, a species is fast if max(P, L) exceeds a threshold δ (about 1e2–1e3 molecules cm⁻³ s⁻¹ was adequate); fast species are solved together implicitly, slow ones by an exponential (QSSA-like) update [31, 32]. The reported gain was 50 % of solver time [31], but rebuilding the local Jacobian offset it [32]. KPP 3.0.0 crops a precomputed Jacobian instead and cut GEOS-Chem solver time by 32 % with mean errors under 1 % for key species; Shen et al. report 30–50 % with precompiled sub-mechanisms [32]. These change the solved system (a mechanism reduction), which may conflict with a "no reduction" rule.
- **Multirate GARK.** Günther & Sandu partition by activity: slow part with step H, fast part with M micro-steps. The paper is theory with no chemistry experiments [28]. I found no chemistry study of gains from partitioned Rosenbrock or multirate methods. Gong & Cho (explicit slow species, implicit fast ones, then a correction) appear only in [7].

### 6. Reduced and mixed precision

- **Atmospheric chemistry.** Sophocleous & Christoudias (2022) report in the abstract that a reworked single-precision solver stayed within 1 % of high-precision implicit methods on seven mechanisms, with 1.5–3× fewer steps, half the memory and up to 2× shorter simulations; I could not read the full text [33]. Their 2026 preprint (not peer reviewed) runs KPP Rosenbrock solvers (ros2 to rodas4) in FP32 in GPU kernels of MECCA/EMAC, converting from FP64 host data. Total species masses differ from the FP64 CPU reference by under 0.004 % (159 species, one month); larger discrepancies occur only for species depleted to near the FP32 range (about 1e-38). The box-model kernel speed-up was 42–45 % (1.42–1.45×) on an A100 (FP32 peak twice FP64); production chemistry time fell about 30 %. No tolerances or mixed-precision linear algebra are given, and the nominal per-species check threshold was a loose 5 % [34].
- **Mixed-precision linear algebra.** Iterative refinement with low-precision LU and higher-precision residuals converges only for matrices well conditioned relative to the factor precision: half-precision LU, single working precision and double residuals need κ∞(A) ≤ 1e4, extendable to 1e8 with LU-preconditioned GMRES [35]. For single-precision LU the limit scales with its unit roundoff *(inference)*.
- **Mixed-precision ODE methods.** Additive Runge–Kutta methods with low-precision implicit stages and high-precision explicit corrections recover full-precision order, with slightly larger errors as stiffness grows; savings were 2.6× to 15× on non-chemistry problems [36].
- **Inexact Jacobian.** W-methods stay stable with an arbitrary Jacobian approximation (abstract) [37]; Verwer et al. give two W-versions of ROS2 [8]. A single-precision factorization acts like an inexact J, whereas classical Rosenbrock methods such as ROS3 and RODAS3 assume an exact J *(inference)*.
- **Not found.** No quantitative float32 or mixed-precision study for stiff combustion mechanisms, and nothing on cancellation in P − Ly. The risk is greatest for near-steady-state radicals where P ≈ Ly *(inference)*.

## Part 3. Open questions and leads

- **LOTOS-EUROS solver:** [11] never names it and the TNO reference-guide URL returned 404; TWOSTEP use is unverified.
- **Unobtained texts:** Hertel (1993), Young & Boris (1977), Mott et al. (2000), Knoth & Wolke (1998), Wolke & Knoth (2000), Kinnison et al. (2007) auxiliary material. Claims about them are second-hand, and MOZART's implicit solver is undocumented in what I could read.
- **Sophocleous & Christoudias 2022:** what "reworked" for single precision means, and why steps fell 1.5–3×, is unknown; read the full text.
- **FP32 tolerances and hardware:** the 2026 preprint states no atol/rtol, so a 1e-3 relative tolerance in FP32 with species near 1e-38 is untested; no source measures the FP32 benefit on CPUs.
- **FP32 LU for chemistry Jacobians (refinement or W-methods):** no source. The conditioning of I/(γh) − J for stiff chemistry (my guess: far above 1e7 unless scaled) needs measuring.
- **Partitioned Rosenbrock or multirate methods for chemistry:** no source with chemistry results; the Sandu group's 2015–2019 GARK/MIS-GARK papers were not read beyond [28].
- **Automatic EBI grouping:** none found; a threshold rule like Lin et al.'s [32] is an untested lead.
- **SIMD structure:** cell grouping by stiffness [21] versus fixed a priori steps [8]: no modern comparison found.
