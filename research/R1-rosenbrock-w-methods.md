# R1. Rosenbrock (ROW) and W-methods for stiff chemical kinetics: literature review

Compiled 2026-10-18; pure literature review, no code downloaded or quoted. [n] = source number in Part 2. s = stages; e = distinct f-evaluations per step; p(p̂) = order (embedded order); R∞ = stability function at −∞; st. acc. = stiffly accurate; W-order = order kept with an arbitrary Jacobian approximation. **(unverified)** and **(conflict)** mark items needing a check.

## Part 1. Findings

### 1.0 Cost model and what the chemistry benchmarks show

- A ROW method with equal diagonal γ does, per step: 1 Jacobian evaluation, **1 LU** of (I/(hγ) − J), s forward/back substitutions, and e ≤ s f-evaluations [1][7]. Jacobian update, LU and backsolves dominate CPU time [1]. Chemistry Jacobians are about 90 % zeros; KPP's sparse LU (Markowitz ordering, no pivoting, no indirect addressing) adds little fill: 276→300 nonzeros (CBM-IV, 32 species), 506→629 (65 species with aqueous phase), 100→107 (17 species) [1][2][4].
- Benchmark of [1]: 7 box models (17–84 variable species), 5-day runs restarted every 60 min (40 min for the smallest) to mimic operator splitting, atol = 0.01 molec cm⁻³, h_min = 0.1 s, h_start = 60 s (problems A–E), accuracy as significant digits (2 digits ≈ 1 %). A sparse Rosenbrock solver was almost always fastest up to ~4 digits; ROS3 and RODAS3 were best up to ~3 digits and order-4 RODAS beyond; RODAS3 stayed within ~25 % of the best at 2–3 digits; it was 5–10× faster than QSSA at 10 % error and 20–100× at 1 %. VODE and SEULEX were >2× slower at 1 % on the stratospheric model (SEULEX ~3× on the wet model). The explicit TWOSTEP was competitive mainly on the 84-species model (and at ~10 % accuracy on the smallest). Stiff accuracy was "not consistently" beneficial; rtol > 0.1 made some Rosenbrock runs drift [1].
- KPP timings (2 GHz PC, 8-day, 212-species MECCA run; tolerances not stated): Rodas3 38.7 s, LSODE 39.8, Ros3 41.3, Rodas4 49.9, Ros4 51.1, SEULEX 55.3, SDIRK 63.2, Ros2 69.4, Radau5 103.3 s [4]. On a 5-species stratospheric toy LSODE (0.32 s) beat the Rosenbrock codes (0.38–0.46 s) [4].
- Reviews agree Rosenbrock methods are most efficient at the low–medium accuracy (0.1–1 %) of transport models [1][7]; Radau and extrapolation win at tight tolerances [1][7][22]. [7] warns that asymptotic error estimators may work poorly at low accuracy.
- Operator-splitting specifics. One-step methods suit the many restarts, but RODAS3 is sensitive to initial transients and wants a small first step [2]. In a fixed-step box-model stress test (clipping on, equilibrium start) the largest stable steps (WET / CBM-IV / RIVM) were 3600 / 3600 / 1200 s for ROS2 with γ = 1+1/√2, 1800 / 1800 / 1200 s for RODAS3 and 150 / 212 / 400 s for ROS2 with γ = 1−1/√2; the authors credit a stability function and internal stability function that are positive on the negative real axis (γ+), and clip negatives to 0 at both stages [2]. Per-cell variable steps cause load imbalance and hamper vectorization [2]; the "vector cell" remedy reduces h until every lane passes [7]; with no Newton loop, ROW methods diverge little across SIMD/SIMT lanes [21].

### 1.2 W-methods and Jacobian reuse

- **Definition.** W-methods have the ROW form but coefficients keep the order for any matrix A_n replacing J [12]. Steihaug and Wolfbrandt (1979) introduced them to keep a Jacobian unchanged over several steps while retaining stability [8][19]. Time-lagged classes T_n = J + O(h) are due to Scholz–Verwer (1983) and Kaps–Ostermann (1988/89) [8].
- **Price of the W property.** At order 3, W-methods need 4 extra order conditions over classical ROW; at order 4, 17 [12]. Order 2 needs only 2 stages (ROS2) [2]. Order-3 W-methods exist with 4 stages (ROS34PW2, ROS34PRW) [11]; ROS3Pw (3 stages) is documented as needing only an approximate Jacobian [14]. A 4-stage third-order W-method cannot also be B_PR-consistent of order 3; ROS34PRw gives up W-order 3 (W-order 2) for B_PR order 3 [11]. For DAE ROW-type methods with arbitrary Jacobian entries, order 3 needs at least 5 stages by theorem and 7 were found [11]; [13] cites this as "s = 7 for a third-order W-method", but ODE W-methods of order 3 exist with 4 stages [11].
- **Non-W methods lose order with inexact Jacobians.** ROS3P and ROS3PRL2 have W-order 1 [11]; even a W-method such as ROS2 drops to order 1 on index-1 DAEs when Jacobian entries are inexact [11]. On Lorenz-96 with a Krylov-approximated Jacobian, Ros4 and Rodas4 fall from 4.0 to ~3.0 while the W-method Rang3 keeps 2.99 [12]. A finite-difference Jacobian preserves order p if its error is ≤ C·h^(p−1) [12]; crude finite differences cut the order of ROK methods to 1–2 on a shallow-water test [12].
- **Time-lagged exact Jacobians** (J from an earlier step, fixed h, scalar coefficients adjusted to keep order; mass conservation is kept because J stays exact) [9][10][1]. In 72 constant-step runs, all stable; accuracy fell once J was frozen (strongly on two problems), so lagging pays only where Jacobian + LU dominate cost [9]. The fourth-order lagged extrapolation code RKRMC used about half the LU decompositions and Jacobian evaluations of ROW4A with nearly equal f-evaluations plus backsolves, but was less accurate at TOL 10⁻³–10⁻⁴ [10]. With variable steps, lagging needs knowledge of the problem's nonlinearity; the authors left this open [9].
- **Chemistry-specific.** [1] lists three approximate-Jacobian options (time-lagged; sparser J, which loses conservation; slow/fast partitioning) and left them for future work. The W-versions of ROS2 in [2] concern transport only (tridiagonal vertical-diffusion A; approximate factorization). [21] (2022) says W-methods "should be investigated" for vectorized chemistry. I found no 2010–2026 study quantifying Jacobian/LU reuse with a modern W-method on atmospheric chemistry, nothing by Wensch on the topic, and only DAE work by Jax and Steinebach [11][13]. PETSc's ROSW needs an extra option to lag the Jacobian [14].

### 1.3 KPP's Rosenbrock integrator

- **Methods.** KPP-2.1: Ros-1, Ros-2 (Verwer et al. 1999), Ros-3 and Rodas-3 (Sandu et al. 1997), Ros-4, Rodas-4 (Hairer–Wanner), plus Radau5, SDIRK4, SEULEX, LSODE(S), VODE [4]. KPP 3.x: ICNTRL(3) = 0 or 4 Rodas3 (**default**), 1 Ros2, 2 Ros3, 3 Ros4, 5 Rodas4, 6 "Rang", 7 Rodas3.1 [5]. Documented (s / f-calls / order): Ros-2 2/2/2(1), Ros-3 3/2/3(2), Ros-4 4/3/4(3), Rodas-3 4/3/3(2), Rodas-3.1 4/3/3(2), Rodas-4 6/5/4(3) [5]. One LU per step ([7]; [5] states it for the tangent-linear variant). [4] states all are L-stable with L-stable (or strongly A-stable) embeddings, and recommends Radau5 for reference solutions.
- **Controller.** After a step, h_new = h·min(q_max, max(q_min, δ·‖l‖^(−1/(p̂+1)))), with l = main minus embedded solution [6]; accepted if the norm ≤ 1 [6]. Norm: RMS over species of (y − ŷ)/(atol + rtol·max(|y_old|, |y_new|)) [6][7] ([1] used |y_new| only). Constants for KPP2 [6, Table 2]: δ 0.9, q_max 6, q_min 0.2, r (factor applied after repeated rejections) 0.1, h_start 10⁻⁵ s; I map these to KPP's FacSafe, FacMax, FacMin, FacRej, Hstart from their descriptions in [5]. [7] quotes 10, 0.1, 0.9 and q_max = 1 after a rejection, the values of [1] **(conflict)**. The KPP 3 docs give no numeric defaults except Hmin = 0 ("not recommended" to change) [5], so KPP 3 values are **unverified**. [1] shrinks h tenfold when the first step is rejected.
- **Tolerances.** ATOL/RTOL are scalar or per-species vectors (ICNTRL(2)); [5] gives no units, and in the cited applications they are molec cm⁻³ [1][6][7]. Applications in [6]: rtol 10⁻², atol 1 cm⁻³ (box model), atol 10 cm⁻³ (EMAC gas phase and SCAV); rtol 10⁻³, atol 1 cm⁻³ (aerosol AERCHEM). [6] cites "as large as" rtol 10⁻², atol 100 cm⁻³ from [7]; [7] says vector, location-dependent atol are needed in 3-D models.
- **Recommendations.** [1]: sparse Rosenbrock solvers are "close to optimal", RODAS3 "never disappointing" for ≤ 2–3 digits. [6] uses Ros3 for gas phase and Rodas3 for aqueous phase, and recommends the second-order H211b controller (Söderlind) as the new default. KPP 3 has a Rosenbrock auto-reduction variant [5][25].

### 1.4 Studies from 2015 to 2026

- **Step-size control [6] (GMD 2025):** the standard embedded estimate overestimates the local error (median SDA 4 vs target 2 in the marine box). Raising δ to 1.3–1.5 (≈V^(1/(p̂+1)) with V the overestimation factor; δ≈1.4) cut function evaluations by up to 31 % (megacity) and 26 % (marine); H211b by up to 31.7 % and 24.1 %. In EMAC, H211b cut evaluations by 43 % (MECCA gas), 27 % (SCAV) and 13 % (AERCHEM); a 1-year run saved >4000 core-hours (11.4 %) vs ≈1500 (4 %) for the retuned first-order controller, with differences well below the tolerance. Changing q_max (6–30) or h_start gave little.
- **New ROW methods:** Rodas5P (8 stages [13]; among the best Rodas-family methods at higher accuracy [20]), Rodas6P and Tsit5DA [13]; Rang's PR methods [14][18]; ROK [12]; RODAS3.1 for KPP/GEOS-Chem (paper submitted 2026, **unverified**) [5].
- **Comparisons:** vectorized ROS3/ROS4/RODAS3/RODAS4 on combustion kinetics [21]: similar at tolerances 10⁻⁴–10⁻⁶; the fourth-order pair slightly faster at 10⁻⁷–10⁻¹¹; ROS4 wins below 10⁻¹¹. A vectorized ROS4 (AVX2, 10 cores) was 12–15× faster than OpenFOAM's ROS4 on the Sandia-D flame, 33–35× with load balancing; OpenFOAM's Seulex was ~2× slower than its ROS4 [21]. [22]: Rosenbrock and BDF dominate at loose tolerances, where the authors' adaptive Radau is "often outperformed" by Rodas5P; Radau wins at tight tolerances (about 1.5–2× faster than Hairer's Radau).
- **GPU (abstract-level):** KPP-generated Rosenbrock kernels: 4.5× (Fermi) and 20.4× (Pascal), 1.75× node-level in EMAC, median relative difference <10⁻⁹ % [23]; CAM4-Chem's second-order Rosenbrock: 11.7× vs one CPU core, 3.82× with transfers [24]; [7] (2011): "faster than multi-core CPU, but by less than a factor of two".
- **Auto-reduction inside the KPP Rosenbrock solver:** 32 % less solver time in GEOS-Chem, <1 % mean error for key tropospheric species [25].

### 1.5 Reading for the design (synthesis, not a source claim)

For 1–2 digits and split steps of 5–30 min the evidence favours 3rd-order L-stable, 3–4-stage methods (ROS3, RODAS3) or ROS2 for very large fixed steps [1][2]; no source tested inexact Jacobians for chemistry (see 1.2); controller constants matter (retuning saved up to 31 % of evaluations [6]).

## Part 3. Open questions and leads

1. **f-evaluation counts** for ROS4 and RODAS conflict ([1]: 4 and 6; [5]: 3 and 5). Hairer–Wanner's coefficient tables (§IV.7) would settle it; I did not read them.
2. **Papers not read** (paywall or 403): [16], [18], [19], [20], Kaps–Ostermann 1989, Rahunanthan–Stanescu 2010, Jax–Steinebach 2017. Whether ROS3Pw is a true third-order W-method for ODEs, and whether ROS3PRL2 is a W-method ([14] yes, [11] W-order 1), is unresolved. Naming is also inconsistent (ROS34PRW vs ROS34PRw; SciML "4th order" for ROS34PW).
3. **KPP 3 defaults** (FacMin/FacMax/FacRej/FacSafe/Hmax/Hstart) are not in the user docs I read; the numbers above are KPP2 values from [6], and [7] quotes different ones. That KPP's "Rang" (option 6) is the Rang3 of [7] is my inference; the RODAS3.1 coefficients and benefit rest on unpublished work.
4. **Journal version of [1]** not read (preprint only). KPP-2.1's Table 2 [4] gives no tolerances. [7] writes the acceptance test as Err ≤ φ_safe, elsewhere it is Err ≤ 1, and calls KPP's "Ros2a/b" stiffly accurate, which does not match ROS2 [2][11].
5. **No chemistry study of W-method Jacobian reuse** after 1999 found; the quantitative evidence is non-chemistry tests from 1980–83 [9][10]. A direct experiment (ROS2, ROS34PW2, ROS3Pw with a lagged or sparsified J on the target mechanism) would close this.
6. **Accuracy beyond 2–3 digits** for split steps of 5–30 min: only [1] (up to ~4 digits) and combustion data [21]; no atmospheric benchmark compares Rodas5P/Radau-class methods at 3–5 digits.
7. **GPU figures** are abstract-level (one from 2011). The 2025 Physics of Fluids OpenFOAM chemistry-solver paper (Rodas34, Rosenbrock34, Seulex) is paywalled; DiffEqGPU (Utkarsh et al. 2024) was not examined. Leads found but not opened: Springer chapter 10.1007/978-3-030-53905-4_6 (order reduction with inexact Jacobians) and Steinebach's RODASP revision.
