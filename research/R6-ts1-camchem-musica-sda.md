# R6 — TS1, the CAM-chem/MOZART/WACCM/MUSICA solvers, and the "significant digits of accuracy" metric

Compiled 2026-10-18. Reading key: **FT** = full text read; **SEC** = relevant sections of the full text read; **AB** = abstract or search excerpt only; **CIT** = only seen cited. Bracketed numbers refer to Part 2. "Inference" marks my own reading, not a source's statement.

## Part 1 — Findings

### 1. MOZART-TS1, MOZART-4 and the CAM-chem solver

**Mechanism sizes (they vary with counting convention and version).**
- Emmons 2020 Table 1: MOZART-1 46 gas-phase species/28 photolysis/112 kinetic reactions; MOZART-2 63/32/135; MOZART-4 85/39/157; MOZART-T1 151/65/287 [1]. TS1 is "T1 with comprehensive stratospheric chemistry" [12] and is CAM6-chem's mechanism (top ~45 km); T1 alone runs in WRF-Chem [1]. TS1 totals are only in the supplement (Tables S1–S2), which I could not get.
- TS1 "contains 221 solution species and 528 chemical reactions (including 405 kinetic and 123 photolysis reactions)", citing [1] [16]. WACCM6's TSMLT1 has "231 solution species, 583 chemical reactions … 150 photolysis reactions, 403 gas-phase reactions, 13 tropospheric, and 17 stratospheric heterogeneous reactions" [8]. ACOM lists TS1 at 216 species and T1 at 179 [12]; MICM's README says "TS1 has 547 reactions" [19].
- MOZART-4: "85 gas-phase species, 12 bulk aerosol compounds, 39 photolysis and 157 gas-phase reactions" [2]; its 2009 preprocessor manual says 97 species, 37 photolysis, 163 gas-phase reactions [11]. E3SM's trop_strat_mozart_mam4: 155 species (130 implicit, 25 explicit), 365 reactions (81 photolysis, 284 gas-phase), Jacobian 1,232 non-zeros (7.29 %) [14].

**Emmons 2020 says nothing about the solver, tolerances or time step** (searched the full text) [1]. MOZART-4 defers: "Details of the chemical solver numerics are given in the Auxiliary Material of Kinnison et al. (2007)" [2]; Kinnison's main text points to that auxiliary material, which I could not retrieve [4].

**The algorithm (CAM6 Scientific Guide §6.4, read verbatim) [9].**
- Per grid cell, dy/dt = P(y,t) − L(y,t)·y, solved by explicit forward Euler "in the case of species with long lifetimes and weak forcing terms (e.g., N2O)" and a "more robust implicit backward Euler method" for stiff species with short lifetimes and strong forcing (e.g., OH).
- "Each method is first order accurate in time and conservative." The chemistry step "is fixed at 30 minutes".
- The implicit system is solved by Newton–Raphson with the Jacobian and "a sparse matrix solution algorithm"; the iteration index "has a maximum value of ten".
- Termination: "when the given solution variable changes in a relative measure by less than a prescribed fractional amount", set per species, "typically 0.001", but 0.0001 for species such as O3.
- If the iteration maximum is reached for any species, "the time step is cut in half" and the solve repeats; it "can be reduced five times before the solution is accepted". No error estimator and no behaviour after the fifth reduction are described. Inference: halving is triggered by non-convergence, not by a local-error estimate, so accuracy is set by Δt rather than a tolerance.
- The guide credits [SPDIC96], [SVvL+97] and [BOT99]. I take SVvL+97 to be [24]; SPDIC96 is probably Sandu, Potra, Damian-Iordache, Carmichael 1996, J. Comput. Phys. 129, 101–110 (unverified).

**Independent description of the same preprocessor-generated solver (ECHAM-HAMMOZ MOZ1.0) [13].**
- "all reactions are treated with the semi-implicit (Euler backward integration) solver" using "LU decomposition and Newton–Raphson iteration"; up to 11 iterations; relative error below 1e-4 for O3, NO, NO2, NO3, HNO3, HO2NO2, N2O5, OH, HO2 and below 1e-3 for the rest.
- Without convergence after 11 iterations the step is halved, up to five times, then a warning is logged. In a 3-day test no halving occurred; convergence took 2–6 iterations, most near sunrise and sunset.
- Blocks of columns are solved together and the threshold is "evaluated for the entire block", so results depend on vector length. CAM-chem likely does the same (inference).
- MOZART-4's manual: "Iteration limit = 11 … maximum number of Newton-Raphson iterations … defaults to 10" [11]. The 10-versus-11 difference across [9], [11], [13] is unresolved.

**Explicit-versus-implicit split [10][11][12][5][6].**
- Three preprocessor classes. *Explicit* (forward Euler): species "presumed to have rather gentle chemistry with longer lifetimes"; OH here "would be disastrous". *Implicit* (backward Euler): the "work horse"; "if in doubt put a species in this class". *Rodas* (implicit Rosenbrock): the "cadillac", about twice as expensive, "only … where the implicit solver exhibits repeated converge failure"; per-species relative criteria below 1e-3 "can cause the computational time to increase greatly"; MOZART "completed hundreds of simulation years … without ever using the rodas solver" [10].
- The user assigns species by lifetime [5]. In CAM4-chem Table A1 (my count) 24 of 136 gas-phase species are explicit: CFC/HCFC/halon and CH3Cl/CH3Br/CH3CCl3/CCl4/CH2Br2/CHBr3 source gases, CH4, N2O, CO2, SF6 and the families BRY and CLY. The other 112 gas-phase and all 20 aerosol species are implicit; the 13 artificial tracers are explicit [6]. The explicit set is small and long-lived; all stiff chemistry is backward Euler.

**Time steps.** CAM-chem chemistry 30 min [9]; the CAM4/5-chem model step is also 30 min (stated in the nudging description) [6][7]; MOZART-2 20 min (2001 draft slides) [3]; MUSICAv0 uses "a physical/chemical time step of 30 and 3.75 min" at ~111 and ~14 km [17].

### 2. CAM-chem and WACCM papers
- Lamarque 2012: the preprocessor generates mechanism-specific Fortran with "two chemical solvers, one explicit and one semi-implicit"; "the semi-implicit solver is quite efficient", so use it "unless the chemical species has a long lifetime everywhere"; Table 3 gives the solver per species [5]. No time step, no solver-error estimate.
- Tilmes 2016: 169 species, explicit or semi-implicit by lifetime [6]. Tilmes 2015: only the 30-min step [7]. Gettelman 2019: WACCM6 sizes above; specified chemistry cuts cost "by a factor of 4 due to removal of the TSMLT chemistry"; no solver or tolerance [8]. Schwantes 2022: TS2.1 adds 46 transported and 50 non-transported species to TS1 and costs about 1.6× more [17].
- Direct solver evidence: replacing CAM4-chem's first-order implicit solver by ROS-2 at the same 1800 s step cut the positive summer surface-ozone bias by up to 5.2 ppb in the eastern US at about half the cost; refining the first-order solver to 180 s gave no significant improvement (abstract only) [15]. Fu's slides: ROS-2 at 180 s about 33 % more efficient than the implicit solver at 180 s; 10-year global-mean surface O3 17.10 (implicit, 1800 s), 16.92 (implicit, 180 s), 17.44 ppb (ROS-2, 180 s) [14].

### 3. MUSICA and MICM
- Pfister 2020 is a vision paper: a gas-phase MICM is in the early infrastructure, MOZART mechanisms [1] run through it, MusicBox accompanies MUSICA v0; no solver numerics [18].
- MICM's Rosenbrock variants: two-stage (L-stable, order 2), three-stage (L-stable, order 3, citing "SVB+97"), four-stage (L-stable, order 4), four- and six-stage differential-algebraic (stiffly stable, orders 3 and 4) [19]. MUSICA's `SolverType`: vector- and standard-ordered Rosenbrock (standard is the default) and Backward Euler, plus `CudaRosenbrock`; Python adds DAE4/DAE6 [20].
- Defaults [20]: Rosenbrock relative tolerance 1e-6; absolute tolerances "nothing uses solver defaults" (no number given); h_min = h_max = h_start = 0.0 s; 1000 steps. Backward Euler: relative tolerance 1e-6; `max_number_of_steps` 11; `time_step_reductions` [0.5, 0.5, 0.5, 0.5, 0.1], "five reduction factors applied after failed solves" — parallel to CAM-chem's 11 iterations and five halvings [9][11][13] (inference). The docs I read give no numeric default absolute tolerance.
- No peer-reviewed MICM accuracy or performance assessment found. MICM's CI benchmarks run Chapman and TS1 (timing: 10,000 cells × 30 steps of 30 s) [19][21]. At commit e15898ce the CPU Chapman run took 959 ms (standard ordering) versus 344 ms (vector128); the "TS1 Timing (GPU backend)" suite on the a10 runner gave 102,717 ms (vector1) versus 7,781 ms (vector128), backend unspecified [21].
- GPU: a 2023 SIParCS project ported MICM's forcing-term function to CUDA: average speedup 2.62× with and 66.3× without data transfer (one A100 versus one core; 500 reactions, 400 species) [22]. CAMP/MONARCH (not MICM): up to 9× from multi-cell batching, 1.2–1.7× from GPU kernels [23].

### 4. Significant digits of accuracy (SDA)
- **Part I, eqs. 15–17 [24]** (checked on the page image). Reference y (RADAU5, rtol = 1e-12, atol_i = 1e-15·η_i), numerical ŷ, samples t_n = t0 + nΔt, species k: ER_k = sqrt( (1/|𝒯_k|) Σ_{n∈𝒯_k} ((y_k(t_n) − ŷ_k(t_n)) / y_k(t_n))² ), 𝒯_k = {n : |y_k(t_n)| ≥ a}. SDA₁ = −log10( (1/m) Σ_k ER_k ); SDA∞ = −log10( max_k ER_k ); a = 1.0 mlc cm⁻³; an empty 𝒯_k is skipped; "SDA = 2 means 1% accuracy".
- **Part II, eq. 6 [25]:** only SDA = −log10(max_k ER_k); a = 10⁶ mlc cm⁻³ (tropospheric problems) or 10⁴ (stratospheric); a = 1 gave "nearly the same conclusions"; hourly samples over 5 days (N = 120); atol 0.01 mlc cm⁻³; rtol 1, 3e-1, 1e-1 … 1e-4; negative SDA means error above 100 %.
- **Adequate accuracy, quoted.** Sandu 1997a: "A numerical comparison should focus on modest accuracies, say relative accuracies near 1%. Higher accuracy levels are redundant for the actual practice of air pollution modelling" [24]; implicit solvers win "when approximately two or more figures of accuracy are required". Henze 2007 (global GEOS-Chem, 1-h chemistry step): "we choose to run the Rosenbrock solver at tolerance levels that yield an SDA of ∼1.0"; versus SMVGEARII, week-mean concentrations changed under 10 % for most species, 10–15 % for HNO2, HNO4, N2O5, NO, NO2 and others [27]. Eller 2009: "we normally use tolerances of 10−3 or lower for GEOS-Chem"; one-week Ox, KPP/Rodas3 versus SMVGEARII: mean error 0.05 %, median 0.03 % [28]. Zhang 2011: "target level of relative accuracy … is 0.1%, i.e., about 3 accurate digits"; overall numerical accuracy "on the order of 1%", with numerical errors "one or two orders of magnitude below" it [29]. Shen 2020 (error of an adaptive mechanism reduction, not of a solver tolerance): median RRMS 0.2–0.8 %; NO2 1–10 % at high latitudes, "well within other systematic sources of errors" [30].
- **Variants.** Henze and Eller use the Part II max form, a = 10⁶ mlc cm⁻³, over grid cells (reference RTOL 1e-8) [27][28]. Eller's printed formula omits the square root although the text says "root mean square" (likely typographic; without it SDA doubles) [28]. Zhang 2011 uses an RMS error (threshold 100 mlc cm⁻³; Radau-5A reference, RelTol 1e-10) and never says "SDA" [29]. KPP-2.1 defines no SDA and recommends Radau5 for references [26].
- **Scale (my arithmetic).** 10⁶ mlc cm⁻³ = 1.66e-12 mol m⁻³; 1 mlc cm⁻³ = 1.66e-18 mol m⁻³; 1e-14 mol m⁻³ = 6.0e3 mlc cm⁻³.

### 5. Errors a solver's error should stay below
- **Rate constants [31].** For the 50 reactions studied, 298 K uncertainties run "from 5 % for well-understood reactions to 30 %" (NO2+OH: f(298) = 30 %); the temperature term gives "over 50 %" in the cold upper troposphere; all photolysis rates were given 10 %. IUPAC uses Δlog10 k, JPL f(298) and g. Combined one-σ uncertainties: 10 % (tropospheric O3 burden), 11 % (surface O3), 16 % (OH), 16 % (CH4 lifetime); NO2+OH and O3+NO dominate; ozone radiative forcing 0.412 ± 0.062 W m⁻² (13 %). Christian 2017 (ARCTAS-A, all inputs perturbed together, JPL rate uncertainties included): one-σ 6.8 % for O3, about 27 % for OH and HO2 [32].
- **Splitting [33][35][34].** Santillana 2016: strategies differ "by at most 10%" in a 1-D prototype, similar in GEOS-Chem; evaluating the stiff chemistry last is more accurate; transport numerical diffusion exceeds splitting error. Sportisse 2000 (not read): the stiff operator should be last (secondhand). Philip 2016, operator durations 10–60 min: relative error grows over 5× from shortest to longest; 5–35 % for NOx and secondary inorganic aerosol, under 2 % for O3 and CO below 30 min; traditional (30/15 min) versus 10/5 min: monthly means differ 5–15 % (short-lived) and about 1 % (long-lived), hourly maxima above 50 % and 5 %; coarser spatial resolution costs more error than longer operators [35].
- **Solver error accepted in practice.** At 1800 s, swapping CAM4-chem's solver changes summer eastern-US surface ozone by up to 5.2 ppb [15], so the operational scheme is not controlled to 0.1 % (inference). GEOS-Chem accepted SDA ≈ 1 [27]. Zhang's 0.1 % target is 1–2 orders below splitting errors of about 10 % [29][33][35] and rate-constant uncertainties of 5–30 % [31] (inference).

## Part 3 — Open questions and leads

1. **Kinnison 2007 auxiliary material** (the solver numerics, per [2][13]) and **Horowitz 2003 full text** were not retrieved. MOZART-2's "implicit Euler backward with Newton–Raphson" reached me only as a search excerpt, so treat it as unverified for [3].
2. **CAM-chem convergence details**: per-species versus per-chunk testing (only ECHAM states per block [13]); 10 versus 11 iterations; behaviour after the fifth halving. These live in the imp_sol source, which I was told not to read.
3. **TS1 size**: Emmons 2020 Tables S1–S2 not obtained. Counts of 216, 221, 229 (a 2024 intercomparison, search excerpt only), 231 and 547 differ by definition and version (JPL19 rates arrived in MOZART-T1.2).
4. **MICM**: the numeric default per-species absolute tolerance is not in the documentation I read; a Backward Euler preset named after CAM-chem (`CamChemParameters()`) appeared only in a GitHub issue excerpt; no peer-reviewed accuracy study. A GitHub PR (NCAR/musica #1044, read through a summariser) says the TS1 config then had CO+OH as a zero-rate stub and stubbed heterogeneous reactions, so MICM's "TS1" may differ from CESM's. Check the current config before comparing.
5. **JPL Publication 19-5 and IUPAC** were unreachable from my tools. Obtain the f(298)/g definition (is f a 1σ?) and f for NO2+OH+M, O3+NO, HO2+NO, OH+CO, OH+CH4; [31] is the secondhand source.
6. **Sportisse 2000**: read it for the order-reduction results at large splitting steps.
7. **Citation notes**: "Shen et al. 2020, ACP 20" is GMD 13, 2475; KPP-2.1 [26] and Zhang 2011 [29] do not define "SDA"; Eller's printed SDA formula lacks the root; Part II's DOI is unverified.
