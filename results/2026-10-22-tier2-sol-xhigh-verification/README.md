## The entry

- **Generator:** `tools/specialize.py`, 374 lines of Python, sharing the
  configuration parser and unit conversion of Tier 1's `tools/configure.py`;
  the solver's control lives in a C header, `src/specialized_solver.h` (160
  lines), and the generated arithmetic in `generated/sp_ts1.c`.
- **What it specializes:** MICM's diagonal Markowitz order, every index, the
  reactant occurrences and yields, the negative Jacobian, the fill, TS1's
  1,244 divisions and 6,986 LU updates, and the substitutions, as
  straight-line C in bounded batches and helper functions; fixed-parameter
  rate formulas; the three stages with their passes fused, each element in
  MICM's order. Full groups use constant strides.
- **Where its speed came from:** by its own kernel measurements (its
  SPECIALIZATION.md), generated rate updates (2.4 times), forcing (1.7), and
  the Jacobian (1.6), with the stage fusion; unrolled LU alone was no faster.
  Cell tiles, which helped astra-xhigh and sol-max, were slower here and stay
  off by default.
- **What stays data:** temperature, pressure, air density, concentrations,
  every custom rate parameter, the step length, and the tolerances.
- **The generic library:** unchanged; the benchmark's common code moved to
  `src/bench_common.h`, and the scenario driver and the specialized solver's
  entry points are new.
- **Generated C for TS1:** `generated/sp_ts1.c`, 112,387 lines and 3.43 MB,
  regenerated by `make bench`; the report's count also includes Tier 1's
  `generated/ts1.c` (1,840 lines).

## The same arithmetic as astra-high

sol-xhigh's results are byte-identical to astra-high's
([`2026-10-22-tier2-astra-high-verification`](../2026-10-22-tier2-astra-high-verification/README.md))
in all six TS1 runs (generic and specialized, both inputs, both compilers)
and in 18 of the 20 day-night runs; on the other two, clean-10min with each
compiler, they agree to 15.4 digits. Both generic solvers are byte-identical
to MICM with clang. This is convergence, not shared code:
- the two generators are independent work, with different structure,
  names, and design (`tools/specialize.py`, 374 and 406 lines, share 7
  substantive lines, all argparse boilerplate or one-line idioms);
- neither session read outside its own directory and the three inputs: the
  compliance scan of sol-xhigh's 152 commands finds no path into another run
  and no listing of the run root;
- both entries reproduce MICM's operation order in their generic solvers and
  emit, in that order, rate formulas with literal coefficients, which each
  compiler folds the same way in both.

## Findings

- **sol-xhigh passes all of Tier 2's gate with both compilers,** with MICM's
  step sequence on every day-night scenario.
- **Specialization gains 1.34 times over its own generic solver** at 18
  threads, the second-largest gain so far, after astra-xhigh's 1.51. Its own
  report, 1.32 times, agrees.
- **Against MICM:** 4.37 times its default and 1.62 times its fastest
  configuration; third of the five entries so far, behind astra-xhigh and
  sol-max.
- **The same arithmetic, different speed:** astra-high computes what
  sol-xhigh computes, to the bit on almost every run, and takes 3.23 s against
  2.74 s; the difference is code organization, not arithmetic.
- **The run took 34 min 35 s and 207,782 tokens**, one turn, no compaction:
  the shortest and cheapest Tier 2 run so far.
