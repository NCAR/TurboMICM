## The entry

- **Generator:** its Tier 1 generator, `tools/generate_c.py`, extended by 162
  lines to emit, besides the mechanism model, straight-line forcing and
  negative-Jacobian code and fixed sparse LU and triangular-solve schedules;
  the generic solver (`src/solver.c`) takes them as callbacks
  (`chem_specialized_ops`), and the specialized benchmark and scenario driver
  are in `src/main.c` (273 lines added).
- **What it specializes:** the reaction order, species indices, stoichiometry,
  Jacobian dependencies, symbolic fill, and a factorization schedule in the
  species' natural order (not MICM's Markowitz reordering). Rate constants
  come from the shared generic evaluator and are cached per workspace while
  their inputs are unchanged. By its own measurements on the 100-cell input
  (SPECIALIZATION.md): straight-line forcing and Jacobian 1.46 times the
  generic path, and with the generated LU and solves 3.39 times.
- **Grouping:** its benchmark keeps MICM's default grouping, one group of
  cells per thread, as its Tier 1 benchmark did (16,200 internal steps at 18
  threads); its day-night runs use 32-cell groups.
- **What stays data:** temperature, pressure, air density, concentrations, rate
  parameters, the step length, and the tolerances.
- **Generated C for TS1:** `generated/ts1.c`, model and specialized kernels in
  one file, 31,335 lines and 1.12 MB, regenerated by the build.

## A defect in its scenario statistics

Its scenario program prints solver statistics that cover only the last
scenario step: each step's call to the group solver overwrites that group's
statistics (`src/main.c`, the scenario loop), so the printed totals are the
last step's, summed over the groups: 288 internal steps on diurnal-10min, for
example, against MICM's 90,980 over the day. The solution itself is right: its
results agree with MICM's to 14.07–14.46 digits on every scenario, which a
different step sequence could not give. luna-xhigh's agent found and fixed the
same mistake in its own driver during its run.
