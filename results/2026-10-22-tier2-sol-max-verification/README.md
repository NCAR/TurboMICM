## The entry

- **Generator:** its Tier 1 code already generated TS1's forcing and Jacobian
  (`tools/configure.py`, writing `generated/ts1.c`, 21,455 lines). Tier 2
  adds `tools/specialize.py`, 395 lines of Python, and two C templates of the
  three-stage solver, `tools/rosenbrock3.c.in` (111 lines) and its tiled form
  `tools/rosenbrock3_blocked.c.in` (158), and makes regeneration follow
  included configuration files.
- **What it specializes:** species and parameter indices, the Markowitz order,
  stoichiometry, the negative Jacobian, the fill, and the elimination and
  substitution operations, emitted as straight-line C; the rate formulas
  with their fixed coefficients; the three-stage pipeline. The default LU is
  MICM's Mozart sequence (reciprocals, scalings, ordered updates), split into
  helper functions of one pivot or row each.
- **Four-cell tiles:** the change that made it faster. Inside each 32-cell
  group, a tile of four cells packs its rates, concentrations, and forcing
  into a small workspace, evaluates its Jacobian, factors, and completes the
  three stages, then copies its state and error terms back; the group's
  serial, species-major error sum still decides acceptance and the next
  step. Fully unrolled code without tiles was slower than Tier 1's (886
  against 378 ms on its small benchmark, its SPECIALIZATION.md).
- **What stays data:** conditions (air density included), concentrations,
  every custom rate parameter, the step length, the tolerances, and the cell,
  group, and thread counts.
- **The generic library:** unchanged; the benchmark's input and output moved
  to `src/bench_io.h`, and the day-night driver is new (`src/scenario.c`).
- **Generated C for TS1:** `generated/ts1_specialized.c`, 66,075 lines and
  2.22 MB, regenerated by `make bench`; the report's count also includes Tier
  1's `generated/ts1.c` and the tests' small mechanisms.

## Findings

- **sol-max passes all of Tier 2's gate with both compilers,** with MICM's
  step sequence on every day-night scenario.
- **Specialization gains 1.22 times over its own generic solver** (1.24 over
  its Tier 1 build), at 18 threads. Its own report, 1.26 times its Tier 1
  benchmark (2,330 against 2,928 ms), agrees in ratio.
- **The gain came from four-cell tiles,** as astra-xhigh's did, found
  independently: fully unrolled code without them was slower than its Tier 1
  code.
- **Against MICM:** 4.80 times its default and 1.78 times its fastest
  configuration; second of the four entries so far, behind astra-xhigh.
- **The run took 1 h 42 min and 549,677 tokens**, one turn, no compaction.
