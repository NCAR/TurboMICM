# Sources and use

- A. Sandu et al. (1997), *Benchmarking stiff ODE solvers for atmospheric chemistry problems. II: Rosenbrock solvers*, Atmospheric Environment 31, 3459–3472. https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/125/0125D.pdf and https://doi.org/10.1016/S1352-2310(97)83212-8. Used to motivate work–precision comparisons, sparse direct solves, and comparisons between low-order and stiffly accurate Rosenbrock formulas. The supplied scenario scorer defines the precise accuracy metric used here.
- J. G. Verwer, E. J. Spee, J. G. Blom, W. Hundsdorfer (1999), *A second-order Rosenbrock method applied to photochemical dispersion problems*, SIAM J. Scientific Computing 20, 1456–1480. https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/1252/1252D.pdf and https://doi.org/10.1137/S1064827597326651. Used for the motivation to examine ROS2, its internal stability during chemistry restarts, and approximate Jacobians. No implementation was copied.
- SUNDIALS CVODE documentation, *Mathematical considerations*, https://sundials.readthedocs.io/en/latest/cvode/Mathematics_link.html (accessed 2026-10-18). Used for the general BDF formulation and the separation of nonlinear convergence, local error control, and matrix refresh. Implementation and adaptation policies in this repository are independently written; no source code was read or copied.
- Supplied MICM snapshot e15898ce, especially the Rosenbrock coefficient tables and the TS1 mechanism, and supplied `reference/` and `scenarios/` documentation and tools. These are the inherited numerical and chemistry definitions and the independent evaluation inputs. Existing translated files retain their original attribution.
- R. Dreger et al. (2025), *Optimized step size control within the Rosenbrock solvers for stiff chemical ordinary differential equation systems in KPP version 2.2.3_rs4*, Geoscientific Model Development 18, 4273–4291. https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-18-4273-2025. Used for context on restart/controller costs, the distinction between local tolerance and global accuracy, and the mathematical H211b controller formula (Eq. 6). The independent implementation applies bounded growth, a safety multiplier, and error-history resets at rate discontinuities. No code or pseudocode was copied.
- K. H. Bates et al. (2024), *Impacts of updated reaction kinetics on the global GEOS-Chem simulation of atmospheric chemistry*, Geoscientific Model Development 17, 1511–1524. https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-17-1511-2024. Used as evidence that scientifically relevant chemical changes can be below one percent; numerical accuracy targets should leave room to resolve such changes. No kinetics were changed in this work.
